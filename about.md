I lead a team of seven data and AI engineers at a 3,500-person global manufacturer, building the platforms — and developing the talent — that let an enterprise actually act on its data.

Over the past eighteen months, my team and I have built a unified enterprise data lake on Databricks, consolidating several dozen disparate sources — structured databases, Confluence spaces, SharePoint directories, and internal wikis — into a single foundation the business can self-serve from. I've personally led or built ingestion for Marketing, Sales, Supply Chain, Finance, and Engineering. I led our BI consolidation onto Databricks, which freed our data scientist from patching deploy tools to instead launch the company's first public-facing AI agent. I architected and shipped our first unified customer profile, with advanced segmentation. And I've been a careful steward of our platform spend — cutting roughly $200,000 in annual run-rate cost across AWS ingestion, Databricks orchestration, and BI licensing through value-based source selection, retention policies, Spark job tuning, and BI vendor consolidation.

## How I got here

I started in enterprise systems consulting at Deloitte, where the work was data conversion and test leadership on a large product-administration implementation — the part of a program where the truth about the data comes out. I spent eight years at TeamSnap, a youth-sports SaaS, in three roles that I still think of as one job seen from three sides: lead data and software engineer, senior product manager for marquee partner implementations, and finally data platform lead, building the dbt and BigQuery platform and the first directly monetized data product. Then two utilities-sector roles: analytics engineer at Colorado Springs Utilities, where transformer-loading analytics informed seven figures of avoided capital, and lead data engineer at E Source on NYSERDA's Integrated Energy Data Resource, a statewide utility data platform, where I rebuilt lineage under regulatory scrutiny and coached the team to a 98% processing improvement on the project's most expensive software line item.

The through-line is that I've never been able to stay on one side of the line between the platform and the decision. I build the pipeline and then I use it; I model the data and then I argue about what it says with the person who has to act on it. That habit is why the [field notes](/field-notes/) on this site read the way they do.

## How I lead

What I'm best at is making space and opportunity for the people on my team to do their best work. On my watch, an Associate Data Engineer has grown into our AI Engineering Lead inside roughly a year, and a Data Engineer has become a Sr. Data Engineer who now leads the unified customer profile work. Earlier in my career, a PM I worked alongside is now a Chief Operating Officer; an entry-level Ruby engineer I hired is a tech lead at Shopify; a Director of Marketing I built closed-loop ad attribution for is now a VP of Growth. The trajectories are the most durable record of leadership I have.

What's a little unusual about how I lead a data team: I speak the language of the manufacturer I serve. Our team's metrics live as a mieruka board, andon, and digital obeya — Lean operations practices applied to data operations. That fluency lets me partner credibly with plant leadership, IT executives, and operators, not just analysts. I stay hands-on by design: I still write the hard MERGE, still read the Spark plan, still sit in the vendor negotiation. A small team can't afford a leader who only reviews slides.

## What I'm looking for

Director / Head of Data roles — or a VP of Data & Analytics seat at a company that wants its data leader to be personally engaged in the technical work — remote or in Colorado Springs. The situations I'm best in: a multi-business or acquisitive company that needs its data estate consolidated and governed; a platform bill that has grown faster than the value it delivers; a leadership team that wants AI tied to outcomes and governed like any other production system.
