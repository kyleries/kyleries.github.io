I have spent eighteen years across consulting, a SaaS company, two utilities-sector roles, and a global manufacturer. In each role I have worked on both the data platform and the business decisions it feeds. The [field notes](/field-notes/) cover the specific episodes in detail; this page is the timeline of the roles behind them.

<li>
<span class="timeline-era">Dec 2024 – present · Colorado Springs</span>
<span class="timeline-title">Data Project Lead - a 3,500-person global manufacturer</span>
I lead a team of seven data and AI engineers. My team and I built the enterprise data lake on Databricks - several dozen structured and unstructured sources into one governed foundation - and I led the BI consolidation onto the lakehouse's native BI, which freed a data scientist to ship the company's first public-facing AI agent. I architected the first unified customer profile with advanced segmentation. My team and I absorbed two acquisitions' data estates and retired roughly a dozen systems in nearly two years. I took about $200K of annual platform run-rate out of ingestion, orchestration, and licensing. My team took on the finance-grade operating-expense reporting, which is used from the C-suite down to cost-center managers, and I designed a weighted cybersecurity maturity score that leadership adopted into roadmap planning.

## People

"Measuring" results is my instinct, and the best measure I have found of my own leadership is what happened to the people I led. On my current team, a Data Scientist has grown into the AI Engineering Lead and a Data Engineer has become a Sr. Data Engineer who now leads the unified customer profile work. From earlier roles, a product manager I worked alongside is now a Chief Operating Officer; an entry-level Ruby engineer I hired is a tech lead at Shopify; a Director of Marketing I built closed-loop attribution for is now a VP of Growth; a non-analyst I coached became a Lead Analyst. In short, they get hired or promoted to the roles they aspire to.
