I have spent eighteen years across consulting, a SaaS company, two utilities-sector roles, and a global manufacturer. In each role I worked between the data platform and the business decisions it supports. The [field notes](/field-notes/) cover the specific episodes in detail; this page is the timeline of the roles behind them.

<ul class="timeline">

<li>
<span class="timeline-era">Dec 2024 – present · Colorado Springs</span>
<span class="timeline-title">Data Project Lead — a 3,500-person global manufacturer</span>
Lead a team of seven data and AI engineers. Built the enterprise data lake on Databricks — several dozen structured and unstructured sources into one governed foundation — and led the BI consolidation onto the lakehouse's native BI, which freed a data scientist to ship the company's first public-facing AI agent. Architected the first unified customer profile with advanced segmentation. Absorbed two acquisitions' data estates and retired roughly a dozen systems in nearly two years. Took about $200K of annual platform run-rate out of ingestion, orchestration, and licensing. Carried finance-grade operating-expense reporting consumed from the C-suite to cost-center managers, and designed a weighted cybersecurity maturity score that leadership adopted into roadmap planning.

## People

I think the best evidence of my leadership is what happened to the people I led. On my current team, a Data Scientist has grown into the AI Engineering Lead and a Data Engineer has become a Sr. Data Engineer who now leads the unified customer profile work. From earlier roles, a product manager I worked alongside is now a Chief Operating Officer; an entry-level Ruby engineer I hired is a tech lead at Shopify; a Director of Marketing I built closed-loop attribution for is now a VP of Growth; a non-analyst I coached became a Lead Analyst.
